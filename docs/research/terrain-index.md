# Terrain research index: where open elevation data beats the global 30 m products
Index of the six regional terrain reports (board items i1311-i1314, run 2026-11-12/13). Research only. Every row below is copied from a report's own summary table; cells are copied whole (only the "Best source" column is shortened, and marked "[cut; see report]" when it is); the report and section named in the last two columns hold the evidence, URLs, caveats and footprint/tile-index links. "Beats the global 30 m" means the verdict in the report is NATIONAL-OPEN or SUBNATIONAL-BETTER, which here includes anything finer than Copernicus GLO-30 that is open for use (some at only 10-20 m, some DSM-class, some contours only, and some with a free login). Rows whose finer data is paid, request-only, login-gated without a free route, or unclear (RESTRICTED, NATIONAL-PAID) are left out; they are in each report's own tables, as is every GLOBAL-ONLY jurisdiction. Where a source row says "not stated" or "not read" for the spacing, the report did not establish it. Read the licence in the report before caching: several open sets are non-commercial (a personal offline cache is allowed, redistribution is not).

## 2. Global baselines, in one line each
Open baseline everywhere: Copernicus GLO-30 (30 m DSM, EGM2008, free licence; details, withheld-country history and the AWS bucket gap in terrain-rest.md 2.6). Polar: ArcticDEM v4.1 2 m (north of about 51 N) and REMA v2 2 m (Antarctica) in terrain-rest.md 2.5. Open pipeline already carrying many national sources: Mapterhorn (REST 2.6 and 3.4; EUROPE-W 4.2).

## 5. Carried over from the first survey, not re-verified
These come from the first survey (RESEARCH-TERRAIN.md, written 2026-11-12 from background knowledge and search snippets) and are not in the regional reports. Every line is background knowledge, not checked in the regional runs; treat as guidance, not evidence.

Builder guidance (background knowledge)
- Source hierarchy by confidence: national lidar DTM of 1 m or finer, then 2-5 m national or regional sets, then 10 m (NED-class, TINITALY), then Copernicus GLO-30, then GLO-90. Merging needs per-region polygons.
- Datums differ and must be converted to one reference before merging: EGM2008, NAVD88, CGVD2013, EVRF2007, PL-KRON86, DHN2016, JGD2011 and others.
- DTM vs DSM: GLO-30, NASADEM, AW3D30, ArcticDEM and REMA are DSMs (tree tops and buildings); lidar-derived national products are DTMs. Seams at borders show a canopy-height step (up to 10-30 m in forest), so blend, smooth, or fill with FABDEM (non-commercial) where forests and buildings matter.
- Build at a chosen target resolution (5-10 m) and read COG range requests (USGS 3DEP, NRCan, LINZ, Geoscience Australia, GLO-30 are COG/AWS-hosted) instead of mirroring raw 1 m; most European Land/national portals serve zipped 1 km tiles with no range reads.
- Coverage figures and "open" status change as programmes grow (France LiDAR HD, Spain 3rd coverage, NZ, Scotland, Japan DEM1A, 3DEP): read a coverage index at fetch time rather than hard-coding extents.
- Where nothing beats the baseline (old section 8): take GLO-30; optionally FABDEM; AW3D30 or PALSAR 12.5 m as cross-checks.

Size estimates (background knowledge, marked uncertain in the old file)
- ArcticDEM: about 600 TB of strips; 2 m mosaic about 20-30 TB (100 km tiles of 50,000 px, about 1.2 GB each); 10 m and 32 m mosaics about 1-2 TB. REMA 10 m mosaic about 230 GB. FABDEM about 500 GB or more. GEBCO about 7 GB.
