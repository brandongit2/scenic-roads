# Highest-resolution terrain data for Africa, Australia, New Zealand, the Pacific, the polar regions, and the global baselines
Run date 2026-11-13 (board item i1314). Research only. Method: four area sub-agents (Africa north and west; Africa east, central and south with the Indian Ocean islands; Australia, New Zealand and the Pacific; polar regions with the global baselines and aggregators), each told to cite only pages or API answers fetched with a 2xx answer in the run; User-Agent `scenic-roads/0.1 (personal offline map)`; no data downloads. Already covered elsewhere and only pointed to: Hawaii, Guam, Northern Marianas, American Samoa, Alaska (terrain-usa.md), Canary Islands, Madeira, Azores (terrain-europe-w.md), Svalbard and Iceland (terrain-europe-e.md; Svalbard's Norwegian Polar Institute licence was re-verified here, section 3.4). Verdicts: NATIONAL-OPEN, SUBNATIONAL-BETTER, RESTRICTED (finer data exists but is paid, request-only or licence-blocked), GLOBAL-ONLY. Sections 2-6 are grouped per area in the same order: 1 Africa north and west, 2 Africa east/central/south and Indian Ocean, 3 Australia/NZ/Pacific, 4 polar and global.

## 1. Headline

- New Zealand is the one place in this report with open, nationwide data far finer than the globals: LINZ's nz-elevation holds 1 m DEM and DSM (a national composite plus 224 survey collections, captured 2008-2026, CC BY 4.0, anonymous COG/STAC on AWS); the gaps are the Chatham Islands, the Pacific dependencies (Cook Islands, Niue, Tokelau) and, tested against LINZ's published capture-area polygon, Fiordland (west and south) and the interior of Stewart Island; the old claims of gaps south of Taupo or across the southern South Island are not supported (every town and range tested is inside; sections 2.3, 3.3.12). Regional councils need no row of their own: their lidar is inside that collection list.
- Australia: Geoscience Australia's national 5 m lidar DEM (CC BY 4.0, a patchwork of 236 surveys 2001-2015) and the ELVIS portal serve most state lidar free under CC BY. Open at 1-2 m or better: NSW, South Australia (Adelaide 2018), the ACT (2020), Tasmania (2 m statewide, CC BY 3.0), Queensland (free once three years old; newer data bought) and Norfolk Island (0.5 m). Victoria is split: 46 of the 322 Vicmap 1 m DEM footprints are "Open" via ELVIS (about 30,000 km2 of footprint area), 276 are licensed through Vicmap's resellers (its 10 m and coastal 2.5 m DEMs are CC BY). RESTRICTED: Western Australia (ELVIS serves WA only the 1 s and 5 m grids; Landgate lidar is paid, A$154.10 down to A$68.85 per km2, under a "Personal Use License"). Christmas and Cocos 1 m DEMs are CC BY-NC (fine for a personal cache, not for redistribution). Northern Territory has GA's 5 m plus the Kakadu 2011 1 m lidar (CC BY 4.0); Darwin's 1 m was not found. ELVIS itself could not be read (it serves only its JavaScript shell), so state coverage comes from the states' footprint services.
- Africa has almost nothing open finer than 30 m. NATIONAL-OPEN: Rwanda 10 m (NLA, CC BY 4.0; probably a DSM from about 2008/09, accuracy unknown), Reunion (IGN LiDAR HD 0.5 m, RGE ALTI 1 m/5 m, Licence Ouverte 2.0) and Mayotte (RGE ALTI 1 m; LiDAR HD not yet there). SUBNATIONAL-BETTER: Cape Town (City Ground Level Map 5 m bare-earth DEM: the City's terms are a disclaimer with no licence clause, the layer metadata adds non-commercial and acknowledgement; the map service returns an 8-bit image, the metre raster is supplied on email request), Kruger National Park (0.25 m DSM/DTM on CEDA, CC BY 4.0) and small research patches: Morocco's Guemassa-Marrakech Pleiades corridor (CC BY 4.0), the Fogo UAV DSM in Cape Verde (10-25 cm, CC BY 4.0), Ethiopian volcano and Namibian/Malawian fault patches on OpenTopography.
- Africa finer but closed: South Africa nationally (NGI's own DEM is 25 m with 5 m contours as a by-product, read via Wayback; the "5 m DEM" is a 2026 research trial; finer grids are commercial: GeoSmart DEMSA2 2 m and SUDEM5, Hexagon/GeoSpace 1 m DSM and 2 m/5 m DTM), Mozambique (1 m Limpopo and Zambezi lidar, 2015-16, about 31,000 km2, World Bank/GFDRR; completion report says public release was not achieved), Accra's Odaw lidar (budgeted in a World Bank document, no release), the Nile Delta coastal DEM SRI18 (terms unknown), Gauteng and the Western Cape (no open DEM or lidar; contours only), Johannesburg (lidar >= 4 pts/m2 on request), Durban (2 m contours open on the eThekwini hub, no lidar or DEM), Dakar and six Senegalese urban areas (PGIIS lidar, portal unreachable), Lagos State lidar (news report only), St Helena (2014 Pleiades DEM, portal marks it "Not Open"), Zanzibar, Dar es Salaam, Freetown, Maputo, Beira, Kinshasa, Saint-Louis, Lagos and Douala (donor drone imagery open on OpenAerialMap, no downloadable DSM/DTM grid found). Every other African state and territory, including the Indian Ocean islands and the French Southern Territories, is GLOBAL-ONLY (section 2.1, 2.2; routes tried in section 5). Digital Earth Africa and RCMRD carry only SRTM/Copernicus copies; Madagascar's "elevation" on open.africa is 1 km SRTM labelled "Not Open".
- Pacific: finer open data was verified only for French Polynesia (lidar 2015 Tahiti urban, Moorea, Bora Bora; cc-by MNTs on data.gouv.fr and Shom Litto3D lidar under Licence Ouverte v2.0) and Majuro (USGS 1 m topobathy, public); New Caledonia has an open 10 m DEM and Litto3D-NC lidar is announced; the FSM has 10 m DEMs for Chuuk, Kosrae and Pohnpei. Lidar certainly exists and none has a public download for PNG, Vanuatu (2012-13 and 2022), Samoa (2012, 2015), Tonga (2012, 2021, 2026), Tuvalu (2019), Kiribati (2023-24), Palau (two 2021 surveys), the Cook Islands (2022 contract) and Fiji (Moana platform). The Pacific Data Hub is behind a Cloudflare challenge that was not bypassed; SPREP, OpenTopography, Digital Earth Pacific, HDX, data.gov.au, data.gouv.nc and USGS TNM were checked instead and hold none of it (section 5.3).
- Polar: ArcticDEM v4.1 (2 m mosaics, CC BY 4.0, ellipsoidal heights, anonymous S3/STAC) is the best open DSM for all land north of about 51 N, Alaska strips after June 2022 excepted; REMA v2 (2 m, CC BY 4.0) covers Antarctica and the sub-Antarctic islands to -53.5 (the Norwegian Polar Institute has no Dronning Maud Land terrain model, only vector maps and a 20 m Peter I Oy DTM). Greenland has a Danish 2 m composite surface model (CC BY 4.0; FTPS needs a free login, but Mapterhorn republishes it as PMTiles). Norwegian Polar Institute: Svalbard 5 m (part) and 20 m (all land), Jan Mayen 5 m, Bouvetoya 20 m, all CC BY 4.0. BedMachine is bed topography, not surface.
- Global baselines (section 2.6): Copernicus GLO-30 (30 m, EGM2008, DSM, free licence; Armenia, Azerbaijan and Moldova released Dec 2023, though the AWS bucket still lacks the 25 Armenia/Azerbaijan tiles; product page says "maintained until 2026") is the open baseline. NASADEM/SRTM/ASTER/AW3D30 are free and older. FABDEM V1-2 (60 S to 80 N, EGM2008) is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; MERIT is CC BY-NC or ODbL and coarser; TanDEM-X 90 m is scientific use only; GEDTM30 is CC BY 4.0. Mapterhorn is the open pipeline carrying 151 national sources (its z0-12 planet file 355.6 GB, z13+ about 9.9 TB); OpenTopography's DEM API needs a free key; AWS Terrain Tiles are a frozen composite.
- Legal and licence traps found: Landgate (WA) personal-use licence; Victoria 1 m fee licence; CC BY-NC on Christmas/Cocos DEMs and on New Caledonia's SRTM layer (CC BY-NC-ND); OpenTopography's copy of ArcticDEM restricts the 2 m product to education and research (use PGC's own route); Cape Town's DEM is non-commercial; the Azores lidar licence disagrees between Mapterhorn (non-commercial) and EUROPE-W (CC BY 4.0), and Madeira's resolution/licence differs between Mapterhorn (10 m) and EUROPE-W (2 m/5 m) (section 6.1).
- What stays unverified (section 5): NGI's price and licence, the Pacific Data Hub's 5 m DEM records, Dakar's PGIIS, Ethiopia's SSGI lidar terms, Darwin's 1 m lidar, the ELVIS dataset catalogue (unreadable), Queensland's lidar licence version, and the BAS item page for the Antarctic Digital Database (403, whose data is vector anyway).

## 6. Accidental findings

### 6.1 Africa north and west

- Licence conflict for the Azores lidar: Mapterhorn's EXCLUDED.md says "Azores, Portugal: Non-commercial license", while terrain-europe-w.md records the Governo dos Acores lidar as CC BY 4.0 via a login. Worth re-checking before caching.
- Mapterhorn's Madeira source ("ptmadeira") is a 10 m MDT of the island attributed to IGP and CC BY 4.0, whereas EUROPE-W records a 2 m / 5 m DROTe set "uso sem condicoes".
- HDX "DTM" search noise: IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix datasets share the acronym.
- OpenTopography point cloud licence for Guemassa (CC BY 4.0) is unusual for a catalogued 2026 patch of Africa: the only African open Pleiades-derived topography in the area.
- Mozambique's 1 m Limpopo/Zambezi lidar (about 31,000 km2 together; World Bank/DFID-GFDRR P149629) is the largest finer-than-GLO-30 African set found in the whole task, and is closed (section 2.2).
- energydata.info holds CC0-1.0 rooftop-solar building vectors for Accra, Lagos, Dar es Salaam, Nairobi and Addis Ababa from 0.5 m stereo imagery: footprints only, no heights or DSM published.
- The World Bank Data Catalog API is rate-limited (HTTP 429) on every call; the World Bank Documents API (search.worldbank.org/api/v3/wds) is the working route. The old Kenya Open Data hub subscription is cancelled (401).
- OAM Dar es Salaam includes a 2018 Maxar city mosaic under CC BY-NC 4.0.

### 6.2 Africa east, central and south, Indian Ocean

- IGN LiDAR HD programme page says the DROM except Guyane are due "d'ici 2026"; the WFS hit counts show Reunion has 2,665 tiles but Mayotte none yet, while Mayotte has 1 m RGE ALTI via Mapterhorn. ZICAD zones are withheld from lidar-derived grids (confidential).

- The Hexagon South Africa 1 m / 2 m national model (announced 2022-2026) is paid and would be the only finer-than-global national grid in Africa, but is not open.
- Cape Town's City Maps will supply the 5 m DEM and 2 m contours free of charge on email request, for non-commercial use with attribution; the contours are also downloadable by bbox from the Open Data Portal.
