# South American bathymetry: Brazil, Southern Cone, Andean and northern countries, Falklands and South Georgia (oceans, coasts, lakes, reservoirs, rivers)
Run date 2026-11-13 (board item i1317, task #1317). Research only. Method: three area sub-agents (Brazil; Southern Cone incl. Falklands and South Georgia; Andean and northern countries incl. Galapagos and French Guiana), then a second pass of three more (academic margin multibeam and GEBCO contributors; lakes and reservoirs; rivers and institutions) whose findings are folded into the tables as rows 47 to 79 and in-place edits, each told to cite only pages or API answers fetched in the run; User-Agent `scenic-roads/0.1 (personal offline map)`; files were fetched whole only where a row names what was read from them (one 44 MB PANGAEA grid was read for its header only); other large files were sized by HEAD or range request. Merged by hand into one table per region; each product has one row. Global products (GEBCO, GMRT, ETOPO, DCDB, Navionics) are in bathy-global.md and only referred to here. URLs are cited inline in the cells (section 8 lists them all). "Not stated" means the page fetched did not say.

## 1. Headline

- South America has very little open, measured, national bathymetry. No national hydrographic office (Brazil DHN/CHM, Argentina SHN, Chile SHOA, Peru DHN, Colombia DIMAR, Ecuador INOCAR, Uruguay SOHMA, Venezuela, Guyana, Suriname) was found publishing a downloadable sea-floor grid; they publish paper charts and ENCs, give data to GEBCO/Seabed 2030, or release on request (SHOA: online form with a signed commitment, no fee, row 18; Brazil LEPLAC: Brazilian university researchers only, row 2; DIMAR: 17 "batimetr*" records in its CECOLDO catalogue are PDF maps, row 77). The backbone is GEBCO_2026 with its TID grid (bathy-global.md row 1).
- Where GEBCO's TID will show measured cells: GEBCO's contributor list (two grids old, GEBCO_2024; text under table 2.4) names Brazil DHN (a national digital terrain model of the whole continental margin, 2021), Argentina SHN (South Atlantic multibeam and Hesperides-cruise grids), Chile SHOA (two multibeam cruises in the Pacific, two single-beam south of 50 S), Uruguay SOHMA (one single-beam set), Colombia CIOH (one Southern Ocean cruise only), and for Peru, Ecuador and Venezuela only their ENC soundings; Guyana, Suriname, Paraguay, Bolivia, the Falklands and South Georgia are not on it (BAS and the Southern Ocean centre cover the latter). NCEI/GMRT survey counts by coast (row 79): Chile best covered, Brazil south of the Amazon, Uruguay/Argentina, Peru and the Guianas thin. Expect the TID to be mostly predicted cells off Peru, the Colombian Pacific, Venezuela and the Guianas.
- Best open sea-floor sets (all licence-read): Chile margin and fjords, Schmidt Ocean Institute FKt241011 grids 10-50 m, CC BY 4.0 (row 55) and Sea of Chiloe grids, CC BY-NC-SA (row 56); N Chile forearc 75 m, CC BY 4.0 (row 57); SO297 Copiapo/Taltal 50 m, Zenodo CC BY 4.0 (row 58); Baker-Martinez fjords 3.6 arc-second, CC BY 4.0 (row 20); Ewing Terrace, Argentine margin, 100 m, CC BY 4.0 (row 59); Vitoria-Trindade chain 40 m from the Brazilian Navy's Cruzeiro do Sul, CC BY 4.0 (row 48); Espirito Santo + Campos 93 m, CC BY 4.0 (row 3); Amazon estuary 30 m, CC BY 4.0, interpolated from chart soundings (row 6); French Guiana, Shom 0.001 deg (~111 m), Licence Ouverte (row 31); Galapagos 100 m compilation 97W-86W, CC BY-NC-SA (row 33) and 2023 EM124 grids (row 34); South Georgia 100 m and South Sandwich 200 m, OGL v3 (rows 25, 26); IBCSO v2 500 m south of 50 S, CC BY 4.0 (row 24); Falklands Sentinel-2 satellite-derived depth, CC BY-SA (row 27). SOI's other Falkor (too) cruises off Argentina, Uruguay, Chile and Peru: raw multibeam is public on R2R without a login (tens to hundreds of GB per fileset, row 64) and processed swath on MGDS (row 63), but neither is in NCEI's multibeam layer nor, for the 2025 Argentina/Uruguay cruises, in GMRT's cruise list (row 79). No Santos, Pelotas, Amazon-fan or Colombian Pacific grid was found; the login-gated PANGAEA sets (row 61) are not open.
- Best inland: ANA's "Cota x Area x Volume" bathymetry, 133 Brazilian hydro reservoirs (Furnas, Sobradinho, Tucurui, Balbina and others) plus 32 semiarid acudes (row 47) as file geodatabases with a reservoir DEM, free HTTP, "o acesso ao dado e livre" (row 1; zips of 0.2 to 14 GB, so cache selectively). The 133 lack Itaipu, Belo Monte, the Cantareira system and the non-hydro supply reservoirs (list in row 1). Titicaca: only a CC0 raster interpolated from contours (row 37) and ALT's 2021 chart PDF from the 2016-2019 echosounder survey, a chart image with no stated licence (row 73); the 2024 Puno Bay multibeam exists only in a magazine article (row 38). Patagonian glacial lakes: CC BY point files (row 15); Laguna del Laja 100 m (row 19).
- Rivers: nothing open for the Amazon, Solimoes, Parana-Paraguay, Magdalena or Orinoco main channels (rows 50 to 53, 69, 70, 75, 76). Only Madeira survey reports (PDF), a SWOT-derived river cross-section product behind a login (row 52), a modelled 100 m Amazon floodplain set (CC BY-NC), and a Sao Francisco reach in USGS DS 965 (792,645 ADCP/RTK points, row 53). Argentina's Port of Buenos Aires channels are survey-plan PDFs under CC BY 4.0 (row 69). ANA's HidroWeb cross-sections have an API gated by a registered ID (not tried).
- Measured-coverage indexes found: ANA's reservoir records (XML lists every zip); BAS shapefiles of contributing surveys for South Georgia; IBCSO's survey-outline geopackage and source list; GEBCO TID; the GeoSGB `geomar` MapServer (Brazilian marine sector maps and 13,848 isobath polylines, row 8); PANGAEA's Elasticsearch bbox search and R2R's cruise API for academic cruises (rows 55 to 64); NCEI's footprint layer and the GMRT cruise list (row 79). No national survey-footprint layer was found for any South American country.
- Open for a personal offline cache (licence read): Shom Guyane (Licence Ouverte), PANGAEA/Zenodo/Mendeley/figshare CC BY items, Dryad Titicaca (CC0), BAS grids (OGL v3), IBCSO (CC BY 4.0), SOI grids on MGDS (CC BY 4.0 for the Chilean Margin grids). Open but share-alike or non-commercial: SAERI Falklands (CC BY-SA 4.0), Galapagos and legacy MGDS compilations (CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 US), Sea of Chiloe (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). Open with no licence text: ANA reservoirs, RIGEO/SGB items ("open"), the NOAA STAR offshore-Brazil CDF. Not usable: Brazil LEPLAC (university affiliation), SHOA (request and commitment), DIMAR, SHN, SOHMA, INOCAR (intranet; paper charts), Peru DHN (paper charts and a copyright-reserved raster chart sale), Garmin/Navionics (global report).
- Nothing found: Venezuela (Maracaibo, Orinoco, Guri), Guyana, Suriname, Bolivia (Poopo only as a 2013 satellite-derived paper), Paraguay (Itaipu, Yacyreta), Uruguay (surveyed, not published), Chilean DGA lake studies as grids (reports only, row 66; Llanquihue, Villarrica, Ranco, Rapel are old limnology titles), Nahuel Huapi, Lago Argentino and Fagnano (paper figures, row 67), Mirim, the Rio de la Plata (CARP monthly surveys unpublished), Brazilian coastal-state data. COPLA, LEPLAC and the UN CLCS summaries publish maps and limit coordinates, not soundings (rows 54, 71).
- EMODnet's grid stops at 11 N (WCS bounds lon -70.5..43, lat 11..90): it reaches almost no South American water (row 43). IBCCA has no grid of its own (row 44). IBCSEP offers two preliminary 1:1,000,000 chart sheets as PDFs and no grid (row 45).
- Earlier survey (RESEARCH-BATHYMETRY.md): its South American claims were two generic statements and both were confirmed (section 7). Not confirmed: IBCSEP as a data source.

## 3. Notes per jurisdiction

- **Brazil.** The Navy's shelf/slope multibeam (LEPLAC) is gated to Brazilian university researchers (Portaria 13/2018, via the Wayback copy of the BNDO access page).
