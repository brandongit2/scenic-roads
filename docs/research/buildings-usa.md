Run date 2026-11-13, two rounds. Read-only research (task #1323) for Scenic Roads. Method: eight sub-agent briefs in all (round 1: national/global, Northeast+Midwest, South, West+AK+HI+territories; round 2: Midwest/Northeast gaps and cities, South/West gaps and cities, Overture's data sampled, national items), each fetching pages and API answers (ArcGIS REST counts and field lists, Socrata, Hub API, item JSON, GitHub, papers, parquet range reads) with User-Agent `scenic-roads/0.1 (personal offline map)`; I read every merged row. Table columns: jurisdiction/product | product, version | publisher | holds | heights (how measured, meaning) | completeness | year | format and access | licence; offline cache; ODbL-mixable | footprint/coverage-index URL | vs Overture | evidence URLs. Counts are the publishers' (returnCountOnly or metadata) as read on 13 Nov 2026. Verdicts: BETTER = measured heights, roof shape or LoD2 beyond what Overture carries; SAME = already an Overture input or equal; WORSE; NEW = a source Overture does not list. Sections: 3 national/global, 4 Northeast+Mid-Atlantic+Midwest, 5 South, 6 West+Alaska+Hawaii+territories (each with tables, notes, a "nothing better found" table, accidental finds and URLs), 7 the Overture sample, 8 the one Unverified list.

## 1. Headline

- **Nothing national beats Overture's measured heights in the USA.** Microsoft's US footprints (no heights) and Global ML footprints (ML heights, MAE 2.5-3.1 m against 3DEP lidar; ~1 M new Vexcel-derived heights per 2026 refresh) are already inside Overture. GBA (RMSE 5.3 m, heights CC BY-NC), 3D-GloBFP, WSF3D, GHSL and Open City Model (2019, regression heights) are WORSE. Google Open Buildings and its 2.5D set do not cover the USA. Cesium OSM Buildings, Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles, Vexcel and Esri 3D Buildings (Master Agreement: no storing, no open-database mixing) forbid an offline cache; Nearmap, EagleView and Maxar-Ecopia are commercial.
- **Measured lidar is the lever, in two forms.** (1) NGA's "133 US cities" lidar buildings: public domain, 2004-2015, about 110 cities, per-building height above ground, average/min/max roof height, roof type and slope (Pittsburgh read in full: 431,583 buildings). Section 4's NGA row lists the cities and where the shapefiles sit (USGS rockyweb, AWS requester-pays bucket). (2) FEMA/ORNL USA Structures (146,381,550 polygons in the live layer, CC BY 4.0) has a lidar-mean HEIGHT in metres only where its SOURCE = NGA, i.e. the same 133-cities data: 31,926,171 of 131,811,952 rows (24.2 %) in the Dec-2024 GeoParquet (CA 44.7 %, FL 33.6 %, TX 25.0 %, NY 24.8 %, IL 23.5 %, DC 94 %, NV 67 %, SD 1.2 %; none in USVI, Guam, American Samoa; PR 19.6 %). The USGS National Structures Dataset repackages the same polygons; NSI's `bldheight` is a copy of that height. The raw 3DEP point clouds behind Overture's "USGS Lidar" heights are the other lever: building class 6 is outside the minimum spec and the WESM index (2,875 work units) has no class field, so coverage is project-specific; own extraction (median roof minus ground per footprint, MAE 1.8-2.2 m in a 2026 USGS study) is TB-scale.
- **Overture's US heights, read from the data (section 7; six small boxes, 54,146 buildings):** only three `sources` dataset names supply a height: OpenStreetMap, Microsoft ML Buildings and "USGS Lidar"; Esri Community Maps, Google and Zenodo never appear. With a height: NYC 98.5 % (OSM 97 %, lidar 2 %), LA 98.9 % (OSM 97 %), Denver 81.9 % (OSM 56 %, lidar 29 %, Microsoft 14.5 %), Houston 89.6 % (lidar 49 %, Microsoft 49 %), rural Iowa 50 % and Nebraska 77 % (all Microsoft, no lidar). NYC/LA heights are the city imports via OSM; lidar heights are patchy; rural buildings get Microsoft's estimate or none. Overture's docs name no US state or city source, and the October 2026 release changed nothing for buildings. National share of lidar heights: not measured (open).

### B. Notes

- **Overture's USGS source.** The attribution page names "USGS 3D Elevation Program Digital Elevation Program" for buildings. The docs do not give the share of US heights per source. The repo's own measure (7 % measured, 36 % Microsoft) is the only share figure.
- **Biggest lever found: measured lidar, in two forms.** (1) NGA's 133-US-cities lidar buildings (see section 4's NGA row, public domain, 2004-2015) and, because HEIGHT in FEMA/ORNL USA Structures is filled only where SOURCE = NGA, that layer's HEIGHT (31,926,171 of 131,811,952 rows, 24.2 %, in the Dec-2024 GeoParquet). (2) USGS 3DEP point clouds: class 6 is outside the minimum spec (LBS 2025 Rev A Table 5) and the WESM index has no class field, so coverage is project-specific; own extraction (median roof minus ground per footprint) is TB-scale. Measured lidar heights beat every estimate set (USA Structures vs 3DEP: R2 0.86-0.87, MAE 1.8-2.2 m, against Microsoft MAE 2.5-3.1 m).
- **FEMA USA Structures:** 146.4 M polygons in the live layer (CC BY 4.0, Phase 3). HEIGHT (lidar mean, metres) exists only where SOURCE = NGA; the live service rejects HEIGHT/SOURCE filters, so exact shares come from the Dec-2024 Source Cooperative GeoParquet: CA 44.7 %, FL 33.6 %, TX 25.0 %, NY 24.8 %, IL 23.5 %, PA 28.2 %, OH 25.9 %, GA 18.6 %, DC 94 %, NV 67 %, SD 1.2 %; none in USVI, Guam, American Samoa; PR 19.6 %. Parquet HEIGHT has no nulls (no height = 0.0). The USGS National Structures Dataset repackages the same polygons (state GPKGs at `prd-tnm.s3.amazonaws.com/StagedProducts/Struct/GPKG/STRUCT_<State_Name>_State_GPKG.zip`).
- **Microsoft Global ML:** the US additions of 2026 (about 1M heights per refresh, derived from Vexcel imagery) arrive in Microsoft's repo before Overture's next release, but they are the same class of ML estimate.
- **Open City Model** is stale (2019) and its heights are a footprint-area regression; do not use.
- **NSI** is a point inventory; its `bldheight` is the footprint source's height in metres (a copy of USA Structures' value), `num_story` and `found_ht` are modelled. Does not beat Overture.
- **Global height rasters** (GHSL, WSF3D, GBA, 3D-GloBFP) are all coarser or ML-estimated; none improves on Overture's measured or floors-based heights in the US. GBA's heights carry CC BY-NC.

### C. Nothing better found

- NGA 133-cities lidar buildings: pages read in section 4 (NGA row); Hub API trap: `hub.arcgis.com/api/v3/datasets?q=` ignores the host, so `<state>.hub.arcgis.com/api/v3` calls return the same global list; the per-site route that works is `https://<hub domain>/api/search/v1/collections/dataset/items?q=...`.

- NGA / OpenTopography 3D models for US cities: pages read: https://portal.opentopography.org/datasets (lidar, DEM, SfM only), a search on NGA/Vricon (commercial, payment required; the search page is not fetched, so this is a search finding only and is listed as Unverified in D too).
- Esri 3D Buildings scene layer, Cesium OSM Buildings, Google 3D Tiles, Vexcel/Nearmap/EagleView/Maxar-Ecopia: restricted or commercial (rows above); none can be bulk cached and mixed into an ODbL map.

### E. Accidental

- The Microsoft USBuildingFootprints repo was archived on 8 Oct 2026; the global repo is now the only live source. Its hosting moved to `https://bfppub.blob.core.windows.net/%24web/<date>/dataset-links.csv`.
- Overture's building theme has 2,529,582,613 buildings (Aug 2026), but Microsoft ML (+13.09M) was the largest July-to-August growth source, i.e. Overture's US share of ML-estimated heights is still growing.
- ohsome shows OSM `height` in NYC (1.07 M of 1.42 M polygons) and Los Angeles (2.96 M of 3.30 M): those two imports are measured city heights already inside Overture; other metros (Chicago, Houston, Seattle, Boston, Phoenix) have almost none, so city-data programmes for those are the targeted gain (other agents' areas).
- Overture 2026-10-21 release exists; the repo pins 2026-09-23.1 (S3 keeps it until 2026-11-25 per the repo docs).

### (B) Notes per jurisdiction

- NYC: the CityGML zip's first file is one 572 MB merged GML of the whole model (DA10_...Merged.gml); a 3 MB range read showed CityGML 1.0, EPSG:2263, per-building roof/wall/ground surfaces. - Cambridge: the city's own page calls it LoD2 or better, citywide download as shapefile/GDB/scene layer package; tiles in SKP, OBJ, DXF.
- Rhode Island: the only statewide LoD1 measured-height layer found in the Northeast (multipatch from the 2022 8 ppsm lidar).
- Vermont: the cheapest statewide measured-height footprint set (HEIGHTFT, 236,262 filled).
- DC: heights come from the 2024 lidar; I3S scene layer plus per-ward Collada downloads are large (1.7 to 6 GB each). Planimetric footprints 2008-2023 are separate yearly layers with no height.
- NGA 133-cities lidar buildings (public domain, 2004-2015): per-building height above ground, average/min/max roof height, roof type and slope; Pittsburgh read in full (431,583 buildings, 2011 flight); the data sit on USGS rockyweb (`NGA_US_Cities/<City>/<project>/VEC/BLD3`) and the AWS requester-pays bucket `usgs-lidar-uscities`. This is the measured alternative wherever a city below has footprints only.

### (B) Notes per jurisdiction

- TX: TxGIO is the item to track. Build a county-by-county pull as TxGIO finishes (ongoing, "continuously updated"); 2,185,538 now, versus Texas' tens of millions of buildings. ### (B) Notes per jurisdiction

- LA County: the best volume find. 3.09 M outlines with HEIGHT>0, stereo-compiled; but 2.37 M rows are the 2008 LARIAC2 capture, only ~0.36 M are LARIAC6 (2020). Heights of newer rows may be better than the old ones; unit/datum of HEIGHT must be read from the gdb field metadata before use. LARIAC4 (2014) layer (1,122,422 outlines, LARIAC4 Building_Footprints on City of LA's services5 host) is the older city copy.
- Portland: RLIS is the richest West-CONUS single source (average, max, min height, ground and roof elevation, roof type for every polygon). Licence page needs a browser to read; resolve before use.
- Oregon SBFO: statewide but heights depend on lidar presence; reports confirm "roof height and lowest ground elevation were derived from lidar, and state lidar coverage is incomplete".
- Seattle: the city has 2015 apex heights, 2023 footprints without heights. No Seattle LoD2 model found. King County's own building layer not located.
- SF: lidar zonal statistics per footprint (hgt_median_m, peak_1st_m): useful for towers via peak, but median is a roof-wide average. The 3D "3D characteristics" blog page returned no text.
- Santa Clara 2020 and Boise 3D are both lidar-measured, post-2019: the two best vintages. Boise adds roof form and is true 3D.
- Phoenix 2014 set is HSIP/USGS: footprints 12 years old but roof type + slope + orientation per building.
- Denver and Jefferson County: stereo-compiled roofprints with heights, 2022. DRCOG's regional planimetric programme (roofprints public domain per its overview PDF, read via a Wayback copy) is the origin; only Denver and Jefferson carry measured heights (Boulder's 2024 outlines have none, Castle Rock's height fields are all 0).
- Bernalillo: 2016 photogrammetric point-cloud heights for 307,333 buildings.
- Montana: only lidar study areas (54 % of 508,834 with heights); index layer gives per-project download zips.
- Colorado OIT "state" layer is OSM, not a state product.
- Hawaii: Honolulu island-wide footprints (239,588, 50 % with 2005/2009 lidar heights) plus the 2013 downtown-to-Diamond-Head CyberCity roof facets are the open sets; Hawaii's statewide lidar DSM/DTM image services (geodata.hawaii.gov SoH_Imagery Hawaii_DSM/Hawaii_DTM, seen in a listing, not queried) would allow heights by subtraction.
- Territories: footprints only (USA Structures), no measured heights.
- Alaska: Anchorage (117,800 buildings, ELEVATION on 93,991, unit not stated) and Mat-Su Borough (58,394 buildings, lidar roof elevations, about 10k usable heights; borough licence bars third-party transfer without authorization, so not ODbL-mixable) are the only height-bearing sets; the statewide footprint item is private (403).
- Sacramento County: a consultant copy of building footprints (item 2017, 204,723 with lidar statistics, but LIDAR_INFO=1 on only 5,170 and absolute elevations) is WORSE.

### (E) Accidental

- USA Structures (FEMA/ORNL/USGS), CC BY 4.0, September 2026, covers every state and territory with Phase 3 geometries; useful as a second footprint source in territories but HEIGHT is sparse (and 0 in USVI/Guam/American Samoa).
- Overture's own docs list no US municipal source, yet counts such as Denver (99.8 %) and LA County (94 %) show how much measured height sits in city layers that Overture's pipeline does not take.
- Open City Model (opencitymodel on GitHub/AWS, 125 M buildings, CityGML/CityJSON/Parquet, 2019) exists as a national LoD1 option, Microsoft-footprint based.
- The Colorado state "Building Footprints" layer is simply OSM; a trap if mistaken for an authoritative source.
- Several county and city services (Albuquerque, Issaquah) have moved or gone token-only since the search engines indexed them.

## 7. Overture's US height sources, read from the data (sample behind the national table's Overture row)

Source: release 2026-09-23.1 buildings parquet, read with DuckDB over HTTP range requests (only `height`, `num_floors`, `bbox`, `sources` columns), one small bbox per place. Schema pages: https://docs.overturemaps.org/schema/reference/buildings/building/ , https://docs.overturemaps.org/blog/2026/09/23/release-notes/ , https://docs.overturemaps.org/blog/2026/10/21/release-notes/ (as cited by the sampling agent).

### What the schema and release notes say

- Building schema page (v2.0.0, updated 2026-09-17): `sources[]` is a list of structs with fields `property`, `dataset`, `license`, `record_id`, `update_time`, `confidence`, `provider`, `resource`, `version`, `between`. The parquet footer shows exactly these. The page's example record has `property` empty and `dataset` OpenStreetMap. `height` = "distance from the lowest point to the highest point" in metres.
- No page lists dataset names for the height property, and no page gives per-source height counts. The buildings guide names OpenStreetMap, Esri Community Maps, Microsoft, Google Open Buildings, an East-Asia ML set, IGN Spain and City of Vancouver; "USGS Lidar" appears nowhere on the guide, only in the data (and, per the earlier report, as a USGS 3DEP entry on the attribution page).
- Release notes 2026-09-23 and 2026-10-21 give worldwide footprint counts by source: Google Open Buildings 898,130,959; OpenStreetMap 706,818,187; Microsoft ML Buildings 689,237,083; Zenodo (doi:10.5281/zenodo.8174931) 212,222,368; Esri Community Maps 15,119,688; IGN Spain 12,298,053; City of Vancouver 16,274. The 10-21 table shows 0.00 % change for every row: the buildings theme was not refreshed in October. Neither table counts heights or says anything about USGS Lidar.

### What the data shows (the structure)

`sources` entries come in two kinds:
- a whole-record entry, `property` = `''` (empty string): the dataset supplied the footprint and whatever attributes it has (OpenStreetMap, ODbL-1.0, with `update_time`; or Microsoft ML Buildings, ODbL-1.0, no update_time);
- a per-property entry, `property` = `/properties/height`: another dataset supplied the height only. Only two datasets appear with it in the US samples: `USGS Lidar` (license null, update_time null) and `Microsoft ML Buildings` (license ODbL-1.0, update_time null).

Example records (sources only, as read):
- `[{p:'', d:'OpenStreetMap', l:'ODbL-1.0', t:'2021-10-12T21:58:24.000Z'}, {p:'/properties/height', d:'USGS Lidar', l:null, t:null}]` (NYC)
- `[{p:'', d:'OpenStreetMap', l:'ODbL-1.0', t:'2024-12-02T21:45:29.000Z'}, {p:'/properties/height', d:'Microsoft ML Buildings', l:'ODbL-1.0', t:null}]` (NYC)
- `[{p:'', d:'Microsoft ML Buildings', l:'ODbL-1.0', t:null}, {p:'/properties/height', d:'USGS Lidar', l:null, t:null}]` (LA: a Microsoft footprint with a lidar height)

So a record whose only entry is OpenStreetMap and which has a height got that height from the OSM `height` tag (or its import); a record whose only entry is Microsoft ML Buildings got the ML height; a record with a `/properties/height` entry got its height from that dataset. Records with no height have no `/properties/height` entry. No other dataset (Esri Community Maps, Google, Zenodo set) appeared in any US sample; not one record in 54,146 sampled rows lists them.

### Row-group, footer and bytes

- Files are 512 parquet parts of about 500 MB each (4.9 M rows per part, 128 row groups of 30,000 to 53,000 rows). The footer is about 0.67 MB per file (read: little-endian length in the last 8 bytes of part-00000 = 671,038).
- Row groups carry bbox statistics, and parts are spatially sorted (the order is a space-filling curve but not a Hilbert curve I could fit), so finding a place means reading footers of several parts: no index maps place to file. I read 33 footers to locate the six boxes (about 22 MB).
- Each region sample itself read 5.0 to 6.3 MiB through DuckDB (footer included; 14 to 18 range GETs; Nebraska 2.9 MiB). Every GET was one footer or one column chunk of a row group, and none was a whole file; only the totals were logged, not each GET's size. The six region samples, the second NYC run and the 10-21 run totalled about 50 MiB, plus about 22 MiB of footer probing, about 70 MiB in all. A repeat is cheaper from a pre-built place-to-file index (a list of each part's first and last row-group bbox costs the 22 MB once).
- A row group of the needed columns is not above 5 MB: the `sources` column is the biggest, a row group of it is a few hundred KB.

### Open questions

1. Samples are one 0.04 to 0.1 degree box per place; shares inside a city vary by neighbourhood (lidar was found in Denver, Houston, NYC, LA but with different shares). A national share of USGS Lidar heights needs a wider read (for example every 20th row group of all 512 parts: footers alone are about 340 MB), which this brief does not allow.
2. Whether Overture's USGS Lidar height is a per-building lidar measure (maximum, median, roof minus ground) or a per-footprint summary is not stated in any page read; `license` and `update_time` are null on those entries.
3. Why Iowa and Nebraska show none while those states have 3DEP lidar: not tested (a coverage overlay with WESM is the next step).

Scratch removed: my files were in `scratchpad/bld1323b/cscratch` and `cscripts`.


**Byte budget:** each request was one footer or one column chunk (none a whole file); locating the six boxes took 33 footer reads (~22 MB) and the samples ~50 MiB (about 70 MiB in all). The FEMA parquet counts in section 7 likewise read ~90 MB of column chunks by range, with no file downloaded. A repeat should build a place-to-file index of the parts' bboxes once (~22 MB) and reuse it.
